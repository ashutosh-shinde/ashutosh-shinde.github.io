Lotka–Volterra competition

α21 — the effect of one SP2 individual on SP1, counted in SP1 individuals; α12 — the effect of SP1 on SP2 (this is the owner's convention; many textbooks swap the indices)

The theory, in short

  • Two logistic species. Each grows logistically alone (ri, Ki); the other species adds a linear drag.
  • Competition coefficients. α21 converts SP2 into SP1-equivalents (α21 = 2: one SP2 individual weighs on SP1 like two of its own); (N1 + α21N2) is the crowding SP1 feels.
  • Equilibria. e0 = (0, 0), e1 = (K1, 0), e2 = (0, K2), and e3 = ((K1 − α21K2)/(1 − α12α21), (K2 − α12K1)/(1 − α12α21)) when it is positive.
  • Stability comes from the Jacobian's eigenvalues at each equilibrium (the README's table: node, saddle, spiral, centre).
  • Invasion tests. SP1 can grow when rare in SP2's world iff K1 > α21K2; SP2 iff K2 > α12K1. Both → coexistence; one → that species wins; neither → bistability (founder control).
  • Coexistence needs each species to limit itself more than it limits the other.
  • No spirals. In competition the Jacobian at e3 has real eigenvalues, so populations approach without oscillating.
  • Growth rates set the speed and the shape of the paths, not the outcome.
  • Degenerate cases. α12α21 = 1 with matching K's gives a line of equilibria (neutral); near it the approach is extremely slow.
