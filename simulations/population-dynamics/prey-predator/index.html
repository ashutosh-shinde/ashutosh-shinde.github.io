The theory, in short

  • General form. Prey: growth without predators minus predation; predators: converted predation minus deaths.
  • Lotka–Volterra (exponential prey, Holling I predation): e1 = (δ/(γβ), r/β) is a centre; orbits are closed curves of V; cycles never decay or grow (neutral, Lyapunov stable but not asymptotically stable).
  • Volterra (logistic prey): predators can live only if K > δ/(γβ); then e2 is stable: a spiral for most values, a node (no swing at all) when K is just above δ/(γβ) or the prey grows very fast.
  • Rosenzweig–MacArthur (logistic prey, Holling II predation): N* = δN0/(γβ − δ); e2 is stable for K < KH = N0 + 2N*, where the top of the prey nullcline lies left of N*; beyond KH (a Hopf bifurcation) a stable limit cycle appears.
  • The paradox of enrichment. Raising K (more food for the prey) destabilises the RM system: the opposite of the Volterra model.
  • Predators lag prey. Predator peaks follow prey peaks (a quarter of a period for small LV cycles).
  • Classification of equilibria from the Jacobian's eigenvalues, as in the README's table (spiral, node, saddle, centre; non-hyperbolic cases need the nonlinear terms).
